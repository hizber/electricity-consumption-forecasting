## Forecast contract

Assume a forecast origin at the end of hour `t`, with consumption through `y[t]` available, and predict the interval ending at `t+1`. Confirm actual measurement latency and assignment split timezone. `MTime` is UTC and denotes the end of the preceding consumption interval; convert to `Europe/Helsinki` for local-calendar features, including daylight saving. See [[Proposed forecast protocol]]. Every feature must be available by the origin, including its publication latency and data vintage.

## Prioritized hypotheses

1. **Consumption history:** `y[t]`, a selection of recent lags `y[t−1]`, `y[t−2]`, etc.; trailing means, extrema, variability and changes over 3, 6, 24 and 168 completed hours. Windows must end at or before `t`.
2. **Daily and weekly patterns:** values 24 and 168 elapsed hours before the target are `y[t−23]` and `y[t−167]`; add nearby lags and prior completed-hour profiles. Fixed UTC lags do not necessarily match local clock hours around daylight-saving changes.
3. **Local calendar:** hour, weekday, weekend, day of year, seasonal cyclical encodings, Finnish public holidays and selected interactions (hour × weekday, temperature × hour). Calendar features for the target hour are known in advance. A holiday may or may not matter for this particular home.
4. **Available weather history:** temperature at or before the origin when actually published; recent 3–168-hour temperatures, trailing means and changes, cold threshold/heating-degree-style terms; try wind, humidity or radiation only when their timing and data quality justify it. Floor heating makes thermal persistence plausible, but it must be validated. The training data's contemporaneous temperature correlation is descriptive, not forecast-time importance or causation. The CSV has 2016 weather despite the PDF's stated 2017 start.
5. **Forecasts for the target hour:** only archived weather forecasts issued and available by the origin. Keep issue time, availability time, forecast valid time, source version and station/grid location; avoid forecast revisions or observed target-hour weather. This is optional if a reliable archive does not cover the needed period.
6. **Other candidates:** sunrise/daylight features based on an appropriately known location; historic day-ahead Finnish prices only if genuinely published in time and a tariff response is plausible. Neither benefit has been established for this home. Station coordinates belong to a weather station, not a verified house address. Do not invent occupancy or appliance status.

Household size and heating information are constant in this single-house dataset, so they are useful context but do not vary by row. `Unnamed: 0` is an exported row index, not a measured predictor. The consumption unit is unspecified.

## Models and model-generated features

- A gradient-boosted tree or random forest can be a strong **non-neural comparison** on lagged tabular features and can help explore interactions. It does not replace each student's required neural-network implementation. Importance is model- and validation-dependent; correlated features can mask one another.
- LLMs can suggest and explain candidates, help write and review causal feature code, and document AI use. They do not create genuine observations of occupancy, appliance use, weather or future consumption. Verify all suggestions and do not use invented numeric features as facts.
- Optional later experiment: use predictions or learned representations from another model as neural inputs, but produce fitting rows with time-ordered out-of-fold predictions and generate each validation prediction using only past-fitted models. Fit all preprocessing and feature selection within the relevant training fold. Avoid in-sample stacking and target/future leakage. Compare the complete pipeline against the simpler model on identical forecast origins.
- Research on three LLM forecasting methods found their LLM components were not necessary for strong results in those experiments; that is not proof about all architectures. A more complex model is a hypothesis, not an accuracy guarantee.

## Public-data sources to investigate

- [Finnish Meteorological Institute open data](https://en.ilmatieteenlaitos.fi/open-data-sets-available): observations and weather forecast products. Verify historic station coverage, publication latency and archived forecast vintages; this page does not establish 2016–2022 historical-forecast availability.
- [Open-Meteo historical forecasts](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-forecast-api) and [previous model runs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/previous-runs-api): potential sources with model-dependent start dates and coverage; inspect how each archived forecast was issued and reconstruct availability rather than using a stitched future-informed series.
- [University of Helsinki Almanac interface](https://almanakka.helsinki.fi/en/name-days/name-day-interface): potential source for Finnish holiday calendars; verify historical coverage, key and licensing terms before use. Calendar features must reflect what was known at each date.
- [Fingrid data FAQ](https://data.fingrid.fi/en/faq): directs users to relevant market-data sources for day-ahead area prices. Confirm Finnish historical coverage, publication time, data licence and whether the household was exposed to those prices.

## Validation plan

Compare identical training-only chronological validation origins: (A) seasonal/persistence baselines; (B) consumption history; (C) add calendar; (D) add past weather; (E) add each external source separately; (F) optional model-generated features. Keep preprocessing, tuning and feature selection inside training folds; compare MAE and RMSE, seasonal/hour slices and stability across more than one time window where practical. Use grouped feature ablations, then optionally blocked/group permutation diagnostics. Correlated features can substitute for each other, so no single importance score proves a feature is useless or causal. Freeze decisions before reporting the assignment's test and three evaluation periods.

| Priority | Candidate at origin `t` for target `t+1` | Reason and availability |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A sequence of the latest 24, 72 or 168 completed hourly consumption readings, ending at `y[t]` | Captures recent behavior; test history length in validation. Requires the latest meter reading to have arrived. Longer history costs more training time and may not help. |
| 1 | Target-aligned daily and weekly lags `y[t−23]` and `y[t−167]`, with nearby historical hours | Gives prior-day/week reference points even to shorter input windows. They are elapsed UTC hours, not always the same Helsinki clock hour across DST. |
| 1 | Target hour and day of week, weekend and season, encoded cyclically or categorically | Known in advance; convert to Helsinki time while keeping UTC timestamps for ordering. Add Finnish holiday indicator as a separately tested candidate. |
| 2 | Rolling means, standard deviation, min/max and changes over completed 3, 6, 24 and 168-hour windows | Summarizes level, volatility and changes; windows must end at `t` or earlier. Raw sequences may already contain this information, so measure whether explicit summaries help. |
| 2 | Latest *available* outside temperature, trailing temperature means and changes; test a heating-degree-style term such as `max(0, 15°C − temperature)` | Plausible for electric floor heating and thermal inertia; 15°C is a trial threshold, not a known property. Check publication latency, missingness and 2016 provenance. Add wind, humidity and solar only in separate ablations. |
| 3 | Archived target-hour weather forecast, if its issue and actual publication time precede origin | Potentially useful but historical coverage for the whole training period is unverified. Never substitute target-hour observed weather for an operational forecast. |

**Published examples:**

- [Wang et al. (2021), residential LSTM and weather](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/10/2737): on 114 apartments, adding apparent temperature to load inputs reduced average MAPE from 53.77% to 48.46% and RMSE from 44.84 W to 41.51 W. One apartment's MAPE worsened from 52.19% to 55.95%. Their aggregate-building result is a different target and cannot be compared directly with a single home. The paper does not establish from the checked description that weather was available at every forecast origin; treat its result as motivation, not a leakage-free performance promise. MAPE values, target scale and conditions also differ from ours.
- [Levikari et al. (2023), Nordic residential buildings](https://lutpub.lut.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/167725/levikari_et_al_load_forecasting_aam.pdf?sequence=1): a TCN experiment used 30 days of load history plus weather and workday information for 48-hour forecasts on 94 multifamily buildings, most district heated. The authors substituted future observed weather for unavailable historical forecasts. This is an optimistic weather-information experiment, not a deployable accuracy estimate. The setting differs from our single electrically heated house.
- [Claessens et al. (2023), household Transformer transfer learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19159): pretraining across households followed by target-home fine-tuning reported approximately 15% lower MAE than its comparison in a limited-data setting. This suggests a possible later experiment, not a guaranteed gain for our several years of target-house training data; inspect all split, scaling and time-alignment details before reproducing it.

## Public data and practical experiment order

- Start with the supplied meter and weather observations; observations do not need downloading again. Check when an observation becomes available. Missing values should use causal imputation, with a missingness indicator if helpful, and all fitted imputation parameters learned within each fitting fold.
- [FMI open data](https://en.ilmatieteenlaitos.fi/open-data-sets-available) may fill a justified weather gap or provide extra observations; check station distance, hourly conventions, time coverage, revisions, and publication latency. The station location in our file is not a confirmed house location.
- [Open-Meteo previous runs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/previous-runs-api) reports that most models start in 2024, GFS 2 m temperature in March 2021 and JMA GSM/MSM in 2018. Investigate actual Finnish coverage, variables and availability latency before relying on any of these. [Single-run archives](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/single-runs-api) may preserve a complete forecast vintage. The [historical forecast series](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-forecast-api) stitches runs, so does not automatically represent a whole day forecast available at one origin. Do not assume an archive covers 2016–2022 completely.
- [UCI individual household dataset](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/235/individual+household+electric+power+consumption) is one French household with minute data and appliance submetering, CC BY 4.0. It is suitable for an independent benchmark or optional transfer-learning investigation, not as actual measurements or appliance features for our Finnish home. Check units, aggregation, licensing and domain differences; disclose source dates and prevent target-house holdout information entering pretraining or feature selection. [UK-DALE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0284) is another multi-home research option with similar transfer limitations.
- Finnish holidays can be derived for the target hour; electricity prices are a lower-priority hypothesis unless the home's tariff and response are known. Historical day-ahead prices can only be used after their actual publication time, with the relevant vintage and historical market rules checked.

**Common comparison:** (1) reproduce the existing previous-day baseline: next-hour validation MAE 0.9181, RMSE 1.2444 on 8,784 hours, in unspecified source units; (2) train all four models with the common history and calendar inputs; (3) compare one added feature group at a time, including past weather; (4) tune input length and model settings only on chronological training/validation folds; (5) freeze choices before the assignment test and evaluation periods. Use the same forecast origins, usable rows and MAE/RMSE, and report missing-data exclusions and training time. Retraining frequency and how newly observed past consumption enters later predictions should be identical or clearly reported. Consider multiple random seeds where feasible.

**Leakage checks:** split by target time before fitting preprocessing; do not randomly split overlapping sequence windows. Earlier observations may provide context for the first holdout prediction, provided they were available at that origin; their labels must not become training targets in the holdout. Avoid centered rolling windows, interpolation that looks forward, scaling or feature selection fitted on validation/test data, and actual target-hour observations. For a 24-hour forecast issued once, later targets cannot use actual consumption from earlier hours in that same future horizon; predict all outputs directly or feed back predictions. Calendar values are known in advance, and weather forecasts must come from an available vintage. Attention weights are not automatically reliable explanations of feature importance or causation; use validation ablations as well.
